# VOICE: how LeoPixels sounds, and what never ships

Every rule here is a pass/fail gate on **post content** — `title`, `description`, `metaTitle`, and every string inside `intro` and `body`, in both languages. One failure is a failed post.

## The voice, from the site's own copy

LeoPixels writes in **second person to the reader** and **first person plural for itself**. This is the single largest departure from the imported BD skill, which mandated third person throughout. The site does not sound like that:

> "You open it on your phone and the number is right there."
> "We build a working demo of your site in 48 hours."
> "Your domain in your name, your files in your hands, any time you ask."
> "A 3am burst pipe, answered in one tap."
> "One Google Ads lead costs more than a month of this."

Read those four sentences before writing. Short declaratives. Literal numbers. Concrete nouns. No hype, no adjective stacks, no throat-clearing.

**"You" is the trades owner. "We" is LeoPixels, used sparingly** — a post is useful writing, not a sales page, so most paragraphs need neither pronoun. If "we" appears in more than two or three paragraphs, the post has drifted into a pitch.

## Rules that hold everywhere

- State facts plainly, in the present tense. "The map pack shows three businesses." Not "it could be argued that the map pack tends to show".
- Every sentence carries information. No filler, no asides, no previewing what is coming.
- Name specific things. A number beats an adjective every time.
- Vary sentence length. Vary how sentences open.
- Explain a term the first time it appears, or drop the term and state the function.
- No fact appears twice. Not in different words, not summarised into an abstract noun later.

## Forbidden constructions

Each kills the wording, not the thought. Restate it legally; dropping the point is its own failure, except for gloss clauses that carry no checkable fact.

- **Shell-noun subject** — an abstract noun summing your own previous sentence, used as the next subject ("That context means..."). State the next fact.
- **False agency** — an inanimate subject plus a causative verb plus an evaluative payoff ("The seamless design elevates the customer journey"). A measurable fact can ride any subject; an evaluative payoff cannot.
- **Fact-gloss** — a clause re-describing a stated fact as its mood or significance. Test: strike the clause; if no checkable fact leaves the post, it was gloss.
- **Metadiscursive importance** — asserting a fact is important or worth attention instead of stating it. If it needs the label, it is not.
- **Reportative evidentiality** — a document as a sentence's subject ("the article shows", "the listing states"). State the fact.
- **Metaphoric identification** — "a hub for", "an oasis of", "a treasure trove". Name the literal thing.
- **Offer / navigation** — any sentence whose job is steering rather than asserting ("For a wider look at X...", "If you want to know more about Y..."). The post never routes the reader.
- **Link-carrier sentence** — a sentence that loses its reason for existing when the link is removed. Every sentence stands linkless.

## Forbidden patterns

| Pattern | Fix |
|---|---|
| En-dash `–` and em-dash `—` | Banned in post content. Use commas, periods, parentheses, or "to" for ranges. |
| Smart punctuation | Curly quotes, `…`, non-breaking space. Use straight `'`, straight `"`, three periods. Both of these are near-perfect AI tells. |
| Invented tricolons | "fast, modern, and reliable" with no real third item. Use three only when there are three. |
| "Not just X, it's Y" | State Y. |
| Participial openers | "Looking ahead", "Bringing together", "Drawing on" are banned outright. Max one `-ing` opener per section. |
| Conclusion-recap reflex | "In short", "Ultimately", "The takeaway", "At the end of the day". Conclusions advance. |
| Throat-clearing | "Here's the thing", "It turns out", "The truth is", "What you need to know". Cut, state the point. |
| Binary contrast | "Not X. Y." / "isn't X, it's Y". Just say Y. |
| Dramatic fragmentation | "Two days. Two stages. That's it." Write the sentence. |
| Rhetorical setups | "What if I told you", "Think about it:". FAQ question headings are exempt. |
| Passive voice | Name the actor. |
| Hedged facts | "seems to", "looks like it might" on a fact in hand. State it. |
| Adverb crutches | really, just, literally, simply, actually, truly, crucially, ultimately. Delete. |
| Business jargon | navigate, unpack, lean into, deep dive, double down, at its core, in today's landscape, game-changer. Plain language. |
| Vocabulary fingerprints | delve, showcase, leverage, harness, elevate, empower, unlock, foster, vibrant, bustling, nestled, tapestry, seamless, curated, robust, meticulous, cutting-edge, transformative. Cut, or state the concrete fact underneath. |
| Scene-setting openers | "Picture this:", "Imagine", "It's a cold morning in...". State the subject. |
| Performative emphasis | "Let that sink in", "Make no mistake", "Period." Cut. |
| Marketing compounds | "customer-centric", "results-driven", "mobile-first" as a boast. Plain descriptors. |
| Timeline filler | "Since its inception", "Over the years". The year, or cut. |
| Non-restrictive relative clause | A fact riding ", which ..." on another sentence. Give it its own sentence. Restrictive clauses that define their noun stay. |
| Fabricated authority | "studies show", "experts agree" with no link. Cite a specific page or rewrite as opinion. |
| Lazy extremes | every, always, never, everyone with no specifics. Real numbers, or "most" / "many" / "few". |
| Closing maxim | A paragraph bowing out on tidy wisdom. End on a concrete fact or a next step. A closer that could end a different post is the maxim. |

## Self-check

Run against every string in both languages. A hit gets fixed in place, and the rewrite re-passes the sweep.

1. Any `–` or `—`? Any curly quote, `…`, or non-breaking space? Rewrite.
2. Throat-clearing opener? Cut.
3. "Not X, it's Y"? State Y.
4. Invented tricolon? Drop the third.
5. Banned adverb, jargon or vocabulary fingerprint? Replace with the concrete fact.
6. Passive voice? Name the actor.
7. A sentence whose subject is a document, or an abstraction under any verb? Rewrite so a person, a company, a place or a concrete thing holds the subject.
8. Three sentences in a row sharing a length, an opening word, or a "The [noun]" frame? Vary one.
9. A fact stated twice anywhere? Cut the repeat.
10. Unsourced authority claim? Cite it or rewrite it as opinion.
11. Any claim about LeoPixels not true on a page a human can open? Delete it.
12. Any client count, rating, testimonial, guarantee or case-study result? Delete it.
13. Delete each link and read the bare sentence. It must still state a real fact and read naturally. A sentence that fragments without its link was written to carry it.
14. Does any paragraph pitch? A post is useful writing. The CTA is rendered by the route, not written by you.
15. Every English heading in Title Case, and every Macedonian heading in sentence case? Question-shaped headings at or under half?

## Scoring

Rate each 1-10. Revise the lowest, re-run the self-check on what you changed, ship at 40/50 or better.

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Directness | Statements, or announcements about statements? |
| Cadence | Varied sentence length and subjects, or the same frame repeating? |
| Trust | No sentence restating another? |
| Authenticity | Does it read as human-typed? |
| Density | Count the sentences deletable without losing a fact. Score = 10 − (2 × count), floor 1. |
